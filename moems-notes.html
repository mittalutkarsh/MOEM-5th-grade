The plan

MOEMS problems look different every week, but one way of working handles all of them. Box the question → Draw every fact → Make the move → Check every fact. Step 3 is where the problems differ: there are thirteen moves, and the picture from step 2 usually tells you which one. One running example walks through all four steps: muffins in boxes.

  1. Box the question: what goes in the answer box, and in what unit?
  2. Draw every fact: one picture that holds every number in the problem.
  3. Make the move the picture shows (undo · bars · table · blocks · gaps · time block · shape · worst case · groups · cycles · digits · odd/even · bound and build).
  4. Check every fact: put the answer back into each sentence. Each one gets a ✓.

Move: Undo it

When only the end of a story is known, walk it backwards: undo each step with its opposite, last step first.

From the official solutions. The official solution to Aiden's secret number (2023–24 E, 4A) says it in one line: work backwards, subtract the 11, then halve.

More worked numbers. Undo 'takes half plus 4' as +4, then ×2: if 24 coins were left, there were (24 + 4) × 2 = 56 before.

Move: Bars and units

Draw every amount as bars of the same size. Find what one bar is worth, then count bars.

From the official solutions. The official solution to Michael and Isabella (2023–24 E, 1D) draws a six-part tape diagram: three parts are the $36 gift, so each part is $12.

More worked numbers. A bar model handles 'after the gift' too: draw the same bars, move some across, and the total stays the same.

Move: Table of cases

When a few choices are possible, list them in order in a table and test every fact on each row.

From the official solutions. The official solutions to Nicole's quarters and dimes (2024–25 E, 1D) tabulate candidate coin counts and then check the winning row both ways.

Move: One block

Find the group that repeats (a set, a pair, a block of time or money), count the blocks, then deal with what's left over.

From the official solutions. The zoo problem's official solution (2025–26 E, 4B): 7 extra students cost $28, so one student is $4, then the group fee is $140 − 15 × $4. LIVE Solve #2A: change $20.25 to 2,025¢ first; then each student is one 25¢ block.

More worked numbers. A pair can be a block: 1 + 59, 3 + 57, … each make 60 (jelly beans).

Move: Gaps and posts

Lengths and waiting times live in the gaps between things, not on the things. In a line there is one gap fewer than posts.

From the official solutions. Maria's stakes (2022–23 E, 2B): the official solution counts three spaces from stake 1 to stake 4 and nine spaces from stake 1 to stake 10.

More worked numbers. Darts: 7 different sums sit on a number line with 6 jumps of 3. Posts and gaps again.

Move: Same time block

When things work at different speeds, pick one block of time they all fit into, count the jobs each does in it, then add (or subtract).

From the official solutions. The official solution to the painting problem (2025–26 E, 3E) adds rates: 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12 of a room an hour. A 12-hour block shows the same thing with whole rooms.

More worked numbers. Clocks: a clock losing 12 minutes an hour is a 'job' of 12 lost minutes per one-hour block.

Move: Draw the shape

Draw the shape to scale-ish and write every length on it. Borders and setbacks come off both ends.

From the official solutions. The pyramid's official solution (2024–25 E, 4D) counts the six views: top and bottom 16 each, each side 10. LIVE Solve #2C: growing a square from side 45 to 46 adds an L of 45 + 45 + 1 squares.

More worked numbers. Moving one side of a rectangle out adds a strip; the strip's area = its length × its width.

Move: Worst case

'Guarantee' means even the unluckiest run works. Build the unluckiest run on purpose, then add one.

From the official solutions. The socks problem's official solution (2025–26 E, 5B) lists the worst-case colour strings before adding the sock that forces the second pair.

More worked numbers. 'At least', 'guarantee' and 'in advance' are the words that call for the worst case.

Move: Friendly groups

Before calculating a long sum or product, look for numbers that make 10, 100 or 1000 together, or a number that repeats. Regroup first, then calculate.

From the official solutions. The official solution to 1974 × 6 + 1969 × 4 + 45 × 6 + 50 × 4 (2019–20 E, 2C) regroups by multiplier: 2019 × (6 + 4). LIVE Solve: (32×46)+(32×34)+(68×80) = 100 × 80, and (56×39) ÷ (14×13) cancels to 4 × 3 before any big multiplying.

More worked numbers. Alternating sums pair up too: 98 − 87 + 76 − 65 is (98 − 87) + (76 − 65) = 11 + 11.

Move: Cycles and remainders

When something repeats, find one block. Throw away the full blocks; the remainder tells you where you land.

From the official solutions. The official solution to the 2019th digit of 5/13 (2019–20 E, 1D) divides 2019 by the block length and keeps only the remainder. LIVE Solve #2E: both remainders are one short of their divisors, so the number + 1 is a multiple of 21.

More worked numbers. If the remainder is 0, you land on the LAST item of the block, not the first.

Move: Digit detective

Puzzles about digits are solved column by column, starting with the ones, carrying as you go. Divisibility rules let you test a digit without dividing.

From the official solutions. The official solution to 5A9 + 5A9 + 5A9 = PLAN (2019–20 E, 1C) starts with the ones column: 27, so N = 7 with a carry of 2.

More worked numbers. A number divisible by 5, 8 and 11 must end in 0 (for 5), and its last three digits must make a multiple of 8.

Move: Odd and even

Whether a total is odd or even depends only on how many odd numbers go into it. That one fact can rule out half the possible answers.

From the official solutions. The official solution to the even-sum squares (2019–20 E, 2B) counts the odd numbers in each square: two odds make an even sum.

More worked numbers. Odd × odd is odd; anything × even is even.

Move: Bound and build

For 'greatest' or 'least', do two things: show the answer can't be bigger (a limit, often area ÷ tile), and build an example that reaches it. Either one alone isn't an answer.

From the official solutions. In the LIVE Solve (Nov 15, 2026), the 4×1 tiles problem (#2D) was solved exactly this way: 35 ÷ 4 = 8 r 3 gives the limit 8, then a drawing shows 8 really fit.

More worked numbers. Least works the same way: show it can't be smaller, then show one example that works.

Tricky bits

Each trap comes from a real problem in the set, with the tempting wrong answer and why it's tempting.

  • Running the story forwards (Feb 16 · The pile of coins)
    Undoing “half plus 4” as ×2 then +4 Undo in reverse: the 4 came last, so it goes back first.
  • Stopping at one guess (Feb 9 · Keri's games)
    “2 wins” leaves 4 points, and seconds come only in 3s. List every case.
  • Forgetting the absent students' sheets (Jan 26 · Mr. Alvarez's paper)
    16 − 3 = 13 The two absent students' 8 sheets come back to the pile too.
  • Counting days instead of jumps (Jan 19 · Jelly beans)
    61 beans on Sept 30 Day 1 to day 30 is 29 jumps of 2, not 30.
  • 40% of the wrong whole (Jan 12 · Ali and Ben's CDs)
    40% of 36 The 40% is of Ali's own CDs.
  • Pairs in the same language (Jan 5 · Two novels)
    15 choose 2 counts two German novels together, which isn't allowed.
  • Ushers don't sit? (Dec 22 · Stadium ushers)
    20,000 ÷ 30 Ushers take places too: a block is 31 people.
  • Only head-on meetings (Dec 15 · Two boats)
    6 passes The fast boat also overtakes once while both go the same way.
  • Dividing the whole bill (Dec 8 · Shipping a package)
    $3.45 ÷ 12¢ The first five pounds cost $1.65 together, not 12¢ each.
  • Subtracting times (Nov 24 · The bathtub)
    20 − 15 = 5 minutes Subtract water (tubs per block), not minutes.
  • Mixing cat and kitten portions (Nov 17 · Cats and kittens)
    3 × 10 − 15 A cat drinks more than a kitten. Draw the quart bars.
  • Forgetting the breaks (Nov 3 · Class periods)
    180 ÷ 4 Four classes still need three 4-minute breaks.
  • The lucky case (Oct 21 · Gumballs)
    3 gumballs (15¢) 'Guarantee' means even the unluckiest machine.
  • Ways vs. sums (Oct 27 · Three darts)
    10 sums 10 is the number of ways to land; some give the same sum.
  • +20% then −20% cancel? (Sep 15 · The computer price)
    $1,000 The second 20% is of a bigger price.
  • Dollars divided by cents (#2A · The quarter fundraiser)
    0.81 students Change $20.25 to 2,025¢ before dividing by 25¢.
  • Growing a square adds one cell? (#2C · The next perfect square)
    N = 1 Side 45 to 46 adds a whole L: 45 + 45 + 1.
  • The limit is the answer? (#2D · 4×1 tiles on the board)
    9 tiles (rounding 8.75 up) Round a limit down, then prove it with a drawing.
  • The biggest three-digit number (#2E · Two remainders)
    999 It must pass both remainder tests; work down from the last multiple of 21.
  • Misreading the sign (Bonus 2 · Cancel first)
    2,366 The slide says ÷, not +. Read every operation sign before starting.

Cheat sheet

When you see…Move
Only the end of the story is knownUndo it: opposites, last step first
“Twice as many”, “3 times”, a percent, a fraction of a wholeBars and units: equal bars, find one bar
A few whole-number choices (wins, digits, coins)Table of cases: list in order, test every fact
“Equal numbers of each”, a price per group, a pair that repeatsOne block: total ÷ block, then the leftover
Things in a row with spaces between (posts, classes, stakes)Gaps and posts: gaps = posts − 1
Different speeds or rates working together or againstSame time block: one block, count jobs, add or subtract
Lengths, areas, setbacks, cubesDraw the shape: label every length, subtract both ends
“Guarantee”, “in advance”, “fewest to be sure”Worst case: unluckiest run + 1
A long sum or product to evaluate (usually problem A)Friendly groups: pair to 10/100/1000, or pull out the number that repeats
“The 2019th digit”, “what day”, a remainder, a clockCycles and remainders: find one block, keep only the remainder
Letters for digits, “divisible by”, primes, factorsDigit detective: ones column first, carries, divisibility tests
A total that must be odd or even; “odd”, “even”Odd and even: count the odd numbers; rule out half the answers
“Greatest number of tiles”, “most that fit”, “least that works”Bound and build: a limit (area ÷ tile), then a drawing that reaches it
Money in dollars and cents, mixed unitsChange everything to one unit first (cents), then divide

Glossary

guarantee / in advance
Must work every single time, even with the worst luck. Calls for the worst case.
at least / fewest / minimum
The smallest number that still works. Check that one less fails.
at most / greatest / maximum
The biggest number that still works. Check that one more fails.
percent (%)
Out of 100. 20% of a price is 20 out of every 100 of that price — of THAT price, not another.
rate
How much gets done in one unit of time (tubs per minute, minutes lost per hour).
set back
Must stay at least that far from a line (a street, a lot edge).
congruent
Exactly the same size and shape.
pass (boats)
Being at the same place at the same time: head-on or one overtaking the other.
profit
Money in minus money out.
dozen
12.
digit
One of 0–9. A 'digit' of 18 is impossible: that's the check that kills a case.
equally spaced
Every gap is the same length. Count gaps, not posts.

Write-up habits

  1. Budget the 30 minutes: problems A–C about 4 minutes each, D and E about 8. If 8 minutes pass with no picture, skip and come back.
  2. Before writing the answer, re-read the last sentence: is this the number the box wants, in the form it wants?
  3. Check by putting the answer back into the problem, not by redoing the same working (you'd repeat the same slip).
  4. For 'greatest' or 'least', check the next number too: one more (or one less) must fail.
  5. Use one unit for everything before dividing: cents with cents, minutes with minutes.
  6. Read the question to the end before writing anything, then box the unit the answer box wants (plan step 1).
  7. MOEMS gives 30 minutes for 5 problems with no calculator and no scrap paper: draw small, neat pictures right next to the problem.
  8. Write every number from the problem on your picture before calculating (plan step 2).
  9. Name the move in a word at the top of your work: undo, bars, table, block, gaps, time block, shape, worst case.
  10. Write the answer with its unit, in the answer box.
  11. Check every fact, not just one: put the answer back into each sentence (plan step 4).
